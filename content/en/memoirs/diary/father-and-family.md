*From Tatyana Timofeevna’s manuscript (after the parents met at the Cheka).*

## Marriage

Father was **twelve years older** than mother.  
They were first given a grand flat on Brestskaya — one night only — then offered the **1st House of Soviets** (**Metropol** hotel, cabin 405 or 407).

This was father’s **second marriage**. From the first, a two-year-old daughter **Lelya** remained with him. Mother feared the child’s greeting. Lelya sat on a bed with a red blanket, looked — and said **“Mama.”** That was their meeting. “We children did not know for a long time that Lelya was our half-sister.”

## Children

- **April 1923** — son **Timofey** (named after father).  
- **1926** — daughter **Tatyana** (notebook chart: 21 Apr 1926; family memory also speaks of living to 95 — confirm against documents).

Father loved the children and **bathed them himself**, phoning from work to ask if the water was ready. When the son was born, mother left the Cheka for other state work.

## Home

First Maly Vlasievsky Lane (Arbat), then **Vozdvizhenka**, 4th House of Soviets.  
See [Childhood in Moscow](/en/memoirs/diary/childhood-in-moscow/).

Father served on the housing commission for the “House of Government” and gave up part of a large flat so another small apartment could be made.

## Mother’s party work

Later mother worked in the **Railway district party committee**: instructor, schools section, agitation and propaganda.
