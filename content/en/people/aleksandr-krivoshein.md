**Aleksandr Dmitrievich Krivoshein** (1926–1988) is the **husband** of **[Tatyana Timofeevna Krivosheina](/en/people/tatyana-timofeevna-krivosheina/)**, a design engineer. In the notebook, photo captions and family speech he is almost always **Sasha**.

**Site canon:** **Sasha = Aleksandr Dmitrievich Krivoshein**, her husband. The full name comes from the **genealogy chart** and aligned person pages.

Below is a continuous biography drawn from **Tatyana’s manuscript**, **his father’s autobiography**, and **open archival/reference publications**. Hypotheses and source conflicts are stated openly; there is no literary invention.

{{< archive_note type="source" >}}
**Do not confuse:**

- **D. A. Krivoshein** — **[Dmitry Aleksandrovich Krivoshein](/en/parents/dmitry-aleksandrovich-krivoshein/)** (1905–1979), **father** of Aleksandr.
- **A. D. Krivoshein** — this person, **husband** of Tatyana Timofeevna.
- A grandson also named **Aleksandr Dmitrievich** (b. 1976) is a **different** person; for the living, the site publishes birth **year** only.
- The name **“Sasha”** on front letter **107** is **not** automatically this husband.
- Namesakes (1990s dissertations, modern engineers, business registries) are **not** linked to this biography.
{{< /archive_note >}}

## Engineering work: “Stalproekt” and “Elektroproektstal”

**According to Tatyana Timofeevna** ([study and work chapter](/en/memoirs/diary/18-study-work-reunions/)), by the time of her state exams **Sasha was already working at Stalproekt**; she was kept in Moscow **because Sasha worked in Moscow**.

In **his father’s autobiography** (House on the Embankment museum site), Aleksandr is an **engineer-designer** at an organisation written as **“Elektroproektstal”**.

The published **UMGB Moscow Region review list** (1956) lists him as an **engineer-designer** and **Komsomol** member (within a group; see below).

{{< archive_note type="source" >}}
**How to name the workplace.** Tatyana’s notebook says **“Stalproekt”**. The father’s autobiography says **“Elektroproektstal”**. These are treated as **variant records of one engineering path** (misspelling, subdivision, or imprecise label), not as two rival “canons.” The official institute name linked to design of steelmaking and rolling-mill equipment (commonly “Stalproekt”) appears in archival guides and in the 1989 State Prize listing. Until a personnel file is in hand, the public wording is: *design engineer; in the family — “Stalproekt”; in the father’s text — “Elektroproektstal.”*
{{< /archive_note >}}

## Arrest of 2 September 1949

**According to Tatyana Timofeevna** ([chapter 18](/en/memoirs/diary/18-study-work-reunions/)):

- **On the night of 2 September 1949** Sasha was arrested.
- “What and how, why?” the family **could not understand** at the time.
- Tatyana telephoned **Dmitry Aleksandrovich in Blagoveshchensk**; Sasha’s parents came to Moscow.
- **Every Wednesday** she went to the **Lubyanka** information bureau; she was told an investigation was under way; later she was allowed to pass on **warm clothing** and **money**.
- **D. A.** was removed as chair of the Amur regional executive committee, put in the **Central Committee reserve**, and given a **strict party reprimand “for his son.”**
- In **November or early December** she was told the sentence: **Article 58**, **ten years**. D. A. and Nina Vasilievna went back to the bureau to re-check.
- After investigation Aleksandr was moved to **Butyrka** prison; Tatyana and **N. V.** brought parcels.
- Then **Vorkuta**; the **first letter** came from there.
- Because of her husband’s arrest she **could not** take the intended job (Censorship); she was **out of work for about a year**; later she taught at **School No. 50 in Lyublino** until Sasha’s return.

**According to Dmitry Aleksandrovich’s autobiography** (House on the Embankment museum):

- **April 1946 – June 1949** — chair of the **Crimean** regional executive committee.
- **June 1949** — appointed chair of the **Amur** regional executive committee.
- **August 1949** — most of the **Crimean** party bureau repressed.
- **2 September 1949** — son **Aleksandr** arrested; the father records conviction under **Art. 111**, **ten years**.
- The father was relieved of leading work; until March 1950 he was in the **Central Committee reserve**.
- Son: born **1926**, married, design engineer; in **1954** — **full rehabilitation** and **reinstatement at work**.

### Which article was in the case

Family sources preserve **two** formulations:

| Source | Wording |
| --- | --- |
| Tatyana Timofeevna (manuscript) | **Art. 58** RSFSR Criminal Code, **10 years** |
| Dmitry Aleksandrovich (autobiography) | **Art. 111** RSFSR Criminal Code, **10 years** |
| UMGB Moscow Region review list (1956) | **no article number** in the group line |

Historical **Art. 111** of the RSFSR Criminal Code (1920s–50s editions) concerned **dereliction / negligent performance of duty**, **not** the modern “occupational safety” offence. Known sanctions under Art. 111 were **far shorter** than ten years, so the combination “Art. 111 + ten years” **needs archival checking** (memory error, combined charges, other qualification — **not** published as fact).

Until a **copy of the investigative file** is obtained, the site does **not** treat either 58 or 111 as the **sole established** article.

### Cause of arrest and the father’s career

The timeline is **close**: father’s transfer from Crimea to Amur (June 1949) → repression of Crimean leadership (August) → son’s arrest (2 September) → father’s removal and party penalty.

But the formula “arrested **because of** the father’s high position” / “hostage” / “pressure on the nomenklatura” is **not proven**. Permissible wording: the arrest took place **against the background** of the Crimean leadership purge and **soon brought** severe consequences for the father’s career; whether the son was arrested **as a means of pressure** can be settled only from the case file.

### Archival list (1956)

In the A. N. Yakovlev Foundation publication (list of UMGB cases for Moscow Region, **December 1956**; RGASPI. F. 589. Op. 3. D. 7930…), **Aleksandr Dmitrievich Krivoshein** appears in a group with other engineers (including Polyakov, Govorov, Bykov, Kuznetsov): **design engineer**, **Komsomol** member; the group was arrested between **1 April and 2 September 1949**; group sentences — **8 to 10 years** corrective-labour camp. The document concerns cases **reviewed and discontinued for lack of corpus delicti**.

The table layout does **not** securely yield a personal rehabilitation date, article number, or **individual** term for each person. The list is **not** used as proof of Art. 58 or 111 specifically.

## Camps, work in detention, return

According to Tatyana:

- **Vorkuta**, then the **Semipalatinsk** area (“they were building something there”; he was already **chief engineer of a concrete plant**).
- **June 1954** — **Sasha returned**.
- The manuscript sentence after the offer to “bring his…” is **broken off** — the site does **not** complete it by guesswork.

According to the father: **1954** — **full rehabilitation** and **reinstatement at work** (no calendar day of an order is published without a document).

Family photographs of **summer 1954** (“Sasha returned,” Petrovsky forest, Kosino) are in the [album](/en/photos/notebook-family-continued/) and above.

## After 1954

Open family and museum sources record **reinstatement at work** after rehabilitation. A year-by-year ladder from “engineer” to “department head” **after** 1954 is **not** laid out in Tatyana’s notebook. The title **department head** of the institute for design of steelmaking and rolling equipment (“Stalproekt”) appears in the **published 1989 laureate list** (below) and matches the family’s memory of Stalproekt work.

## USSR State Prize, 1989

**Decree** of the CPSU Central Committee and the USSR Council of Ministers of **30 October 1989**, No. **931**, on USSR State Prizes in science and technology for 1989 (published in the Collected Decrees of the USSR Government; bibliographic lists for 1989).

In the **laureate list** (including MegaBook / 1989 compilations) appears:

**Krivoshein, Aleksandr Dmitrievich** — **head of department** of the State Union Institute for the Design of Steelmaking and Rolling Equipment for Ferrous Metallurgy (the institute commonly known as **Stalproekt**),

in a collective award **for reconstruction of reheating furnaces of rolling mills**, improving efficiency and rolled-product quality.

**Why this is the same person (public case):**

- full **name**;
- **engineering** / metallurgical design;
- Tatyana’s manuscript: **“Stalproekt”**;
- father’s autobiography: design engineer (with a distorted organisation name);
- **death in 1988** and prize in **1989** fit the rule on **posthumous** awards (Statute on USSR State Prizes, CPSU CC and USSR CM decree **21 March 1989**, No. **251** — posthumous award with diploma, badge and money to the **family** is allowed);
- **family confirmation** (Aleksei Krivoshein, 2 August 2026): the award was presented to the **family in the Kremlin** (ceremony place = **family memory** until an official protocol is found).

We do **not** write that Aleksandr **personally** attended the ceremony.

{{< archive_note type="source" >}}
**Attribution status of the prize.** Name + Stalproekt department head + work + years + family award form a **strong** identification. For ironclad personnel linkage a laureate diploma, institute personnel file (RGANTD f. 434) or full decree clause with GARF archival reference is still desirable. On this site the prize is published as **supported by the open laureate list and family context**, with that caveat.
{{< /archive_note >}}
