## Who she was

My mother — **Samsonova Evdokia** (Eva — as her father and husband called her), née **Morozova**, **Konstantinovna**.

She was born on **14 March** (1 March Old Style) **1900** in Moscow.  
Years in the notebook chart: **1900–1981**; buried at **Novodevichy**.

## Her parents

Father — **Konstantin Mikhailovich Morozov**, from the village of **Griboevo**, Klin district, Moscow province. A worker: electrician, plumber, caretaker of a general’s house.

They lived in **two rooms on the ground floor** on **2nd Brestskaya Street**, near the **Belorussky** railway station. The five-storey house, she writes, still stands.

Mother — **Vera Osipovna Solovyova**. Brought to Moscow at thirteen as a nanny in a German family; trained in neatness and order.

## School and 1917–1918

Strict school routine: cold-water washing, inspection, prayer, breakfast, park walks.

**1917.** Finished school; pinned a **red bow** to her coat.  
As a child she dreamed of becoming a **typist** — watched the pharmacy window where prescriptions were typed. Took typing courses.

**1918.** Her father died of the **Spanish flu** after a trip for food. Buried at the Strastnoy Monastery cemetery — “where the Rossiya cinema stands now.” The grandmother did not work. Moscow was hungry and cold.

## Work: Centropechat → Cheka

First **Centropechat**, then the **Cheka** as a typist in the **secret department**.

Hard years: little bread; cutlets from potato peels; cold winters, no trams. Mother **ran to work from Belorussky station to the Lubyanka**; sometimes hauled frozen potatoes home on a sled.

{{< figure src="/photos/dnevnik-tt/str-010.jpg" caption="Page on hunger, the Lubyanka, and meeting father." >}}

## Meeting father

At the Cheka she met her fate — **Timofey Petrovich Samsonov**, head of the secret department.  
They married soon. Father was older.

## Old Moscow (as mother told it)

Horse tram on the Garden Ring; the conductor’s cry at Triumphal Gates.  
“At 6 p.m. Moscow is quieter than water, lower than grass.”  
Samovar, kerosene lamp, grandfather reading *Moskovskie Novosti* aloud.

## 16 October 1941

{{< figure src="/photos/dnevnik-tt/str-015.jpg" caption="Insert: mother’s memory of the Moscow panic, 16 October 1941." >}}

A pasted insert recalls the **panic of 16 October 1941**: crowds eastward, looting, political books thrown from windows; party staff collected for evacuation; Three Stations square packed with people. “If the Germans had bombed the square it would have been a bloodbath. Thank God it did not happen.”
