*Manuscript, squared notebook; the page continues the account of the family / dacha.*

to the right, next to Mama — brother, to the left of  
Papa sat I, and next to me — sister.

The parents worked a great deal, especially  
Papa. At five in the evening Papa came  
home, rested an hour or so, and then again  
work. He would come then when we were already  
asleep.

And then there was the dacha. We lived in  
Serebryany Bor from 1927. They brought me  
there in the nanny’s pram. Mama says that  
at that time there were many crows there;  
they woke me if the pram was put out  
in the garden. The dacha was on the 1st ride  
(line), house No. 2, not far from the circle where  
bus No. 4 stopped. Then it became  
a school. The school was still of pre-revolutionary  
times, large, single-storey, built  
of age-old pines. In summer a camp  
was in the school.

Our dacha too was not new, but  
good, of logs, two-storeyed.  
Our family occupied the first floor.  
There was a large balcony where we dined.  
There stood a huge sofa (of deal boards),  
and also a small (children’s) billiard table,  
on which Papa loved to play; he  
played billiards very well.  
From the balcony one could go into  
the parents’ room, and from it — into  
a large but rather dark one. Here slept  
brother. And from his room there was a door  
into our room with sister. And also from  
this room there was a door that  
led into a small corridor and Grandmother’s  
room. And there was also a small  
cabin for a domestic worker. In the dacha there were  
2 entrances: one — the front, onto the balcony, and  
the other — the back.

On the second floor (the premises there  
were smaller) Papa’s comrades with their families  
always crowded. For a time the  
Lavrukhins[?] crowded there, then the Pavlunovskys, who  
had as nanny the cook Venka, then someone  
else. One summer the family of  
Papa’s driver Novikov crowded with us.

*(in the left margin, opposite the paragraph about the second-floor tenants:)* the Lilyakins[?]

We had a large plot, but it was  
not all cultivated.

The flower bed and kitchen garden — Papa’s pride,  
the work of his hands.  
He laid out a large flower bed where grew  
various flowers: mignonette, daisies,  
carnations, marigolds, snapdragons, spitsy[?],  
fragrant tobacco, sweet peas,

*The sentence breaks off at the end of the page; the rest of the flower-bed list was not recovered.*
