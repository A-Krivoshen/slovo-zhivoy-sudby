## Why this source matters

The article marking eighty years of the [House on the Embankment](/en/places/serafimovich-street/) includes a **conversation** with [Tatyana Timofeevna Krivosheina](/en/people/tatyana-timofeevna-krivosheina/).  
It is an **independent printed** witness in which she is the journalist’s interlocutor: childhood in the complex in the early 1930s, neighbours, courtyards and clubs, family memory of her father [Timofey Petrovich](/en/people/timofey-petrovich-samsonov/), and how the house changed under repression.

The piece **complements** the family notebook but **does not replace** it and is **not** treated as an official archival document.

## What it supports (with care)

- the fact of a **published** 2011 interview with Tatyana Timofeevna;
- her **published memories** of childhood life, clubs, and courtyards;
- memory of how the **atmosphere changed** under repression (including sealed apartments);
- mention of her father in connection with **settling** the house and a housing commission — as the **daughter’s recollection** (cross-checked with [ch. 02](/en/memoirs/diary/02-father-marriage-children/)).

## Cautions

- phrases such as “born with the house” / “still in swaddling clothes” are **journalistic images**, not precise biography (birth **1926**, move **1931**);
- the newspaper’s 1931 office title for her father is **not** taken as established fact without archives; the notebook has a different/uncertain wording;
- “Babiy” beside the father’s surname is **not** added to public alternateName without independent confirmation (the form appears in the notebook);
- Kaganovich as commission head — interview memory only;
- neighbours’ names (including the Pavlunovsky family) — **place context** only; no new person entities;
- **later** entrance/flat details from the article are **not** published;
- this source is **not** used for the career, arrest, or prize of [A. D. Krivoshein](/en/people/aleksandr-krivoshein/).
