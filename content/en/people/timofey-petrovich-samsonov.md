**Timofey Petrovich Samsonov** (birth / family form **Babiy**; underground name **“Foma”**; **9 (21) May 1888** — **28 October 1955**) was a Russian revolutionary, **Cheka–OGPU** official, Soviet party and administrative worker, film-production organiser, and author of the autobiographical narrative **“Beyond the Law”** (*Vne zakona*, 1933); father of [Tatyana Timofeevna Krivosheina](/en/people/tatyana-timofeevna-krivosheina/) and husband of [Eva Konstantinovna](/en/people/eva-konstantinovna-samsonova/).

This page is built from the **family notebook and documents** and **cross-checked** against open reference works (see [Open sources](#open-sources)). Wikipedia and Wikidata are **navigation**, not sole proof. Uncertain points are marked.

Education (handbooks, consistent with the family centenary sketch): rural primary school; Khotyn district school (c. 1906); study at the Khotyn real school.

## Revolution and imprisonment (1906–1914)

From **1906** — Social-Democratic circle in Khotyn; arrest **September 1906**, Khotyn prison, release on bail (family 1988 cutting; handbooks).
From **1907** — among **anarchist-communists**, nickname **“Foma”**; flying detachment “Destroy and Build” (reference tradition; family cutting evaluates the tactics carefully).
Prisons **1907–1910** (Khotyn, Kamianets-Podilskyi, etc.); meeting **G. I. Kotovsky** in Khotyn prison — **family/reference tradition**, no separate archival act on this site.
**May 1909** (Odessa, per handbooks): sentence to prison and Siberian exile.
**Alexandrovsky hard-labour central** — family photograph captioned that father was held there in summer **1910**:

{{< figure src="/photos/samsonov-tp/09-alexandrovsky-central-1910.jpg" caption="Alexandrovsky Central. Album note: “Father was imprisoned here in the summer of 1910.”" >}}

Exile in **Kirensk uyezd** (carpenter, stevedore, mine labour — handbooks); **Bodaybo**, then **Vladivostok** — family and reference route, without publishing precise addresses.

## Emigration and Britain (1915–1917)

Reference line: escape via **China — Japan — India — Algeria — Spain** to **England**; **Liverpool**, dock labour; transport union; “Union of Russian Seamen”; anarchist-communist activity.

**Family document:** [Expulsion Order, 1917](/en/documents/expulsion-uk-1917/) — Liverpool Police Court **7 April 1917**, Whitehall **28 September 1917**; name forms *Tomofi Sainsanov / Samsonov*, also *Michael Nemchinoff* (aliases need cross-check).

{{< figure src="/photos/samsonov-tp/11-expulsion-uk-1917.jpg" caption="British Expulsion Order (Aliens Act 1905). Family archive." >}}

## 1917–1923: Chelyabinsk, front, Cheka

Return via **Arkhangelsk** (handbooks: arrest and dispatch to the Urals). **Chelyabinsk:** labour exchange, Soviet, railway union (handbooks; [Chelyabinsk encyclopedia card](http://www.book-chel.ru/ind.php?what=card&id=2740) — external, site availability uneven).

**Civil War:** registration / military control, then **Special Department of the 3rd Army** on the Eastern Front; **Moscow gubernia Cheka**; **Registration Directorate** of the Field Staff of the RVSR (dates ± months by handbook).

**1920–1923:** head of the **Secret Department** of the Cheka → GPU → OGPU (core of the “security service” layer in family memory and handbooks).

{{< figure src="/photos/samsonov-tp/13-colt-from-dzerzhinsky-1919.jpg" caption="16 January 1919: F. E. Dzerzhinsky presents comrade T. P. Samsonov with his personal Colt No. 75350." >}}

{{< figure src="/photos/samsonov-tp/08-dzerzhinsky-belenky-papa.jpg" caption="Family caption: “F. E. Dzerzhinsky, Belenky, Papa” (papa on the right)." >}}

Entry into the **RCP(b)** — **1919** (handbooks; family cutting links a 19 Feb 1919 *Pravda* letter by anarchist-communist staff of the 3rd Army).

## 1938–1940: removal / arrest

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Handbooks / Wikipedia** | **arrested 1938**, **not convicted**, released **1940** |
| **Daughter’s notebook (ch. 08)** | **1938** — removed from work, months without a post, life “under a sword of Damocles,” loss of some privileges; later work via the Moscow Party committee (some wording uncertain) |

## War and publishing (1941–1955)

Reference line (details differ by edition):

- **1941–1942** — director of the **Moscow prosthetic factory** (wartime production in some accounts);
- **1942–1944/45** — All-Union **Book Chamber**, **Glavlit**;
- **1945–1955** — **Gospolitizdat** (classics of Marxism-Leninism / personnel — handbook wording).

Family memory: illness **1954–1955**, hospital, “Kratovo” sanatorium, death **28 October 1955** ([ch. 10](/en/memoirs/diary/10-notebook-continued-080-089/), [18/19 family chapters](/en/memoirs/diary/18-sons-150-161/)).

## Conflicts and open questions

1. **Death 1955 vs 1956** — family + bust + Wikipedia/Hrono: **1955**; knowbysight/some dictionaries: **1956**. Public: **28 Oct 1955**.
2. **el-history “1988–1955”** — obvious birth-year typo.
3. **1938** — arrest (handbooks) vs removal (notebook): both layers shown.
4. **Rehabilitation** — not confirmed by a document on this site.
5. **EN Wikipedia** is short and partly muddles roles; chronology prefers RU handbooks + family.
6. Father’s **first marriage** (Genya’s mother) — in the notebook; no full page for the first wife.
