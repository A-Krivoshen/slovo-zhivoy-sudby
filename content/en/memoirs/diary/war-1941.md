## 1941. The district committee

War began. At the Railway district committee there was endless work: trains to the front, the wounded.

## 16 October — panic

Mother **saw with her own eyes** the Moscow panic of 16 October 1941: crowds eastward with belongings; looters; political books thrown from windows.

Party activists were sent East. Trucks from the committee; Three Stations square packed. “If the Germans had bombed the square it would have been a bloodbath. Thank God it did not happen.”  
In boxcars — east; with mother went her brother and the housekeeper.

## Evacuation and return

A plane flying low “about to hit us.” A night stop; then **18 km** on foot to the village of **Poshchupovo** near Ryazan; a collective-farm cart for mother and grandmother. “I lay down on the grass in the garden and fell asleep at once.”

Little food — mother and daughter sold things at the market for rye and potatoes.  
A pass named mother only; the daughter was not listed — they went anyway. Grandmother stayed with Aunt Larisa.

## Moscow again

Neighbours occupied rooms; many things gone — “as if Mamai had passed.” Father greeted them **coolly** — “something had gone wrong between him and mother.” Life had to begin again.

The bakery gave bread and rusks for the front; the Babaev factory — syrup.  
Ration cards: mother **worker’s 800 g** of bread, daughter **dependent’s 400 g**.  
School closed — Tatyana entered an external course at the Mining Institute. Mother returned to the district committee.
