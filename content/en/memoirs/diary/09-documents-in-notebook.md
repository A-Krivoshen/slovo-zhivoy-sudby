3. **Center** — handwritten insert:

**Insert.**  
**From meetings with I. V. Stalin.**

Kislovodsk. 1923. I. V. came to  
Kislovodsk for treatment.

“At the end of his treatment I. V.  
made me a proposal to en-  
ter his service as secretary in  
the Apparatus of the CC. To this I replied  
that since Comrade Dzerzhinsky  
has confidence in me and I  
have long worked under his lead-  
ership, before answering  
I should like to consult  
with him on this question. I. V.,  
smiling, said: ‘Well then,  
consult and tell me.’  
(Father remained with Dz.)

1927. Father appointed Administrator  
of the CC of the VKP(b). It happened  
that Father met I. V. in  
the House of Unions. They greeted each other,  
and I. V. said: ‘I know you are a firm  
manager. We need such a one  
in the Apparatus of the CC.’”

4. **Bottom** — crop/fragment of another photograph (man’s suit, lapel with a badge); no text.

5. **Right** — edges of other pasted sheets/photographs (partly cover the grid).

### Printed text of the clipping

**On Dzerzhinsky.**

Although Dzerzhinsky per-  
ished already three years ago, somehow it is hard  
to get used to the thought that he  
is indeed no longer among us. And  
that is understandable. Dzerzhinsky, on the instruc-  
tion of our party and of Vladimir  
Ilyich Lenin himself, carried out such enormous  
difficult practical revolutionary  
tasks as were within the power only of  
a revolutionary giant, which the  
first chekist was. To his  
lot fell the hardest work of  
strengthening the dictatorship of the proletariat in  
our country.

Both as head of the Cheka and as  
head of our national econo-  
my Dzerzhinsky everywhere developed an im-  
mense working tempo and work. He resembled  
a gigantic machine that inces-  
santly hummed, lived under itself and scat-  
tered everything that stood in the way of  
the development of the proletarian revolution in our  
country, that hindered our success-  
ful socialist construction.

Anyone great who had the happiness to work  
beside the late Dzerzhinsky remembers  
with what heat and fury he fell  
upon those comrades who even  
to any degree raised the ques-  
tion of non-fulfillment of party directives,  
[for carrying out / in carrying out?] [them / in?] [capitalism / ?].  
I remember how at one of the meetings of the Colle-  
gium of the Cheka, it seems in 1921, someone  
of the comrades hinted that the CC  
on one question, in this com-  
rade’s view, had taken a hasty decision. Dzer-  
zhinsky so dressed down this comrade  
that the latter did not know where to put himself. In con-  
clusion Dzerzhinsky declared that he  
would not permit conversations about annulling decisions  
of the Party CC to be raised  
at the Collegium of the Cheka. Since the matter concerned one  
of the military questions, it is clear that the de-  
cision of the Party CC had to be  
carried out without reservation. And it was car-  
ried out. This devotion of Dzerzhins-  
ky in carrying out party decisions showed  
itself also in his last speech at  
the July plenum of the CC of 1926, when  
like a lion he threw himself upon the Trotskyist-  
opposition demagogues who tried  
to weaken the monolith, unity and steadi-  
ness of that party of which Dzerzhinsky  
was a faithful son.

Our enemies portrayed Dzerzhins-  
ky as a hard-hearted, bloodthirsty  
sadist, whereas among many  
old and young chekists one did not  
meet people so responsive to  
human suffering. But  
class hatred for the enemies of the working  
class, capitalists, landowners and their  
henchmen sat so deeply in  
Dzerzhinsky that he with all his being as a revo-  
lutionary-Bolshevik ruthlessly de-  
alt with those who hindered the work-  
ing people from freeing themselves from the yoke of capital and na-  
[tionalism? / onslaught?]. Dzerzhinsky also possessed great  
magnanimity. I remember how once one of  
the former railwaymen obtained [the right?] to im-  
personate[?] Dzerzhinsky and demanded  
at the Cheka garage a car as for the chair-  
man of the Cheka. They sent him a car, but as  
a result the false chairman found him-  
self in the Cheka before Dzerzhinsky him-  
self. The blackmailer trembled like an aspen  
leaf. Dzerzhinsky calmly approached  
him and jokingly said: “So it is you  
who thought that Dzerzhinsky — eh?” The poor  
blackmailer begged for mercy, pointing  
to his proletarian origin.  
Dzerzhinsky forgave him and at once  
released him. Subsequently this rail-  
wayman reformed and became an honest work-  
er.

A special trait of character of this  
enormous revolutionary-Bolshevik  
was that in all the difficulties  
that arose in the work he quickly found  
ways out. In the period of the civil war  
[…]  
[…] in the struggle with political counter-intelli-  
gence there were so many hard  
moments when workers toiled almost  
losing their way, having no possibility  
of getting on the track of enemies who every-  
where dug against us. In such cases  
we went to Dzerzhinsky and, following his  
advice and directions, successfully repelled  
the intrigues of our numerous enemies.  
I remember, on the day of the 25th anniversary of re-  
volutionary Marxist [?] and  
the late Marchlewski told in  
a circle of close friends such an episode from  
Dzerzhinsky’s underground work. In War-  
saw two gendarmes burst[?] into  
the flat of the wanted Dzerzhins-  
ky and were to arrest him. They  
ran in with a search. Dzerzhinsky dashed  
out, not keeping to the door. Quickly he  
slipped out the door, clicked the key and  
locked the gendarmes in the room. Thus  
he saved himself from arrest.

Popular courage and resolution, as  
everyone knows, did not leave Dzerzhinsky  
also later, already in the October pe-  
riod, in deciding questions of enormous  
revolutionary and state signifi-  
cance.

Take at least those difficulties that  
stood before our country in re-  
storing industry after  
the imperialist and civil wars.  
Having received as a legacy a ruined na-  
tional economy, our impoverished  
country had by its own forces,  
surrounded by the barbed wire of block-  
ade, to restore industry and  
transport. Human  
efforts were needed in order to develop the work  
of reconstructing our national econ-  
omy on new socialist princi-  
ples. Dzerzhinsky — at the head of the VSNKh  
of the USSR. Carefully studying the needs of in-  
dustry, he leads the economic  
leading cadres, and through them all  
the working masses, along the path of restora-  
tion and reconstruction of the national econ-  
omy. For this work colos-  
sal means are required. They must be found. Here  
too the exceptional qual-  
ities of Dzerzhinsky show themselves. The revolutionary or-  
gan of the VSNKh of the USSR is given a directive to  
build its work on the revision of indus-  
trial institutions and enterprises not from  
formal principles but in essence,  
by recovering materials and money lying  
dead, by seizing non-  
liquid values, by fighting tendencies  
of certain economic managers who  
divert enormous industrial funds  
to needs not directly related  
to it, and so on, and so forth. Work on  
raising labor productivity,  
the struggle for a regime of economy, for simplifying  
and rationalizing the industrial ad-  
ministrative apparatus, and so on, and so forth,  
also belong among the most important ser-  
vices of Dzerzhinsky in raising and re-  
organizing our national social-  
ist economy.

Especially clearly he devoted himself to the question  
of the struggle with private-capitalist  
elements in our country. In one of  
his numerous notes before  
us, the revolutionary workers of the VSNKh,  
Dzerzhinsky set the task of watchfully  
observing that our economic  
organs not fall into the paws of private  
traders, into the paws of nepmen. Dzerzhinsky under  
all conditions always remained on the gen-  
eral Leninist line of our par-  
ty, courageously fought for it and glo-  
riously perished for the party in the struggle with the disor-  
ganizers-Trotskyists.

**SAMSONOV.**

*[Small newspaper type; individual words and column joins on the photograph are blurred — places with `[?]` and `[…]` were not reconstructed for sense beyond the readable.]*

## Dates and places (as in the text)

- **End 1918 – early 1919** — 3rd Army retreats; headquarters in **Glazov**; arrival of Stalin and Dzerzhinsky; Father’s acquaintance with F. E.; first sight of Stalin.  
- **16 January 1919** — certificate: Dzerzhinsky gives Samsonov T. P. his personal revolver of the “Colt” system No. **75350**.  
- **Winter 1920–21** — Father’s first meeting with Lenin (see the collection *They Guarded V. I. Lenin*).  
- **March 1921** — second meeting with Lenin, Comintern Congress, official setting in the Kremlin.  
- **1919 – 1926** — Father’s work with Dzerzhinsky (until F. E.’s death in 1926).  
- **1923, Kislovodsk** — Stalin on treatment; proposal of secretaryship in the Apparatus of the CC; Father remains with Dzerzhinsky.  
- **1927** — Father appointed Administrator of the CC of the VKP(b); meeting with Stalin in the House of Unions.  
- **1927 – 1934** — period “Administrator of the CC of the VKP(b)” (by the caption to the portrait).  
- **20 July 1929** — *Pravda*; Samsonov’s article “On Dzerzhinsky.”  
- **25/VI/1947** — dedication on the verso of the photo: T. P. Samsonov.  
- **1951** — Father showed his daughter the notebook with notes on meetings.  
- **Liverpool** — prison where Father sat “for a speech at an anti-war meeting.”
