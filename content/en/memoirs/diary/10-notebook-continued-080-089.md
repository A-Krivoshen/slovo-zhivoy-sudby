**Sister and brother**  
**My sister**

As I already wrote, there were  
3 children in our family: Genya (1920), Tyoma (1923) and I Tanya (1926)  
Genya was born in Moscow in February 1920 and died in 1941, [?]  
Genya (Evgenia) was Father’s daughter from his first  
marriage. Her mother died, leaving a two-year-old daughter  
and Father. Mama took her as her own. For a long time  
my brother and I did not know that she  
was our half-sister. We grew up together, not  
knowing any difference. We looked alike outwardly too.  
She was talented; she was drawn to  
[?]. At 16 she wrote [verses?]  
[?], dedicated to me.

**Letter to my sister.**

Dear Tanya’s letter I have received.  
It woke all the sadness of mine.  
Your drawings are very sweet,  
Your [?] how I love you.

Your letter is so full of a child’s tenderness,  
And in the hour of longing and of sad thoughts  
I remember your little eyes,  
I love them so, I love them so

More than once we joked and played together,  
More than once we quarrelled over trifles,  
And your voice is so pure and tenderly kind —  
Dearer than the dearest of voices.

Write to me, Tanya, my dear,  
I love your Tanya letters so,  
I read them for rest,  
They do not let me grow bored.

Farewell, dear little sister,  
Only sing and play just so,  
And be as before, my dear,  
Merry, happy, my own.

She was 16.  1 December 1936. Zvenigorod.

This poem Genya sent me from  
a sanatorium where she was treated for tubercu-
